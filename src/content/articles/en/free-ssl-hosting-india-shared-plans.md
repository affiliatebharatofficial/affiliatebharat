## The short answer: free SSL is now a basic feature, not a premium one

If you searched for "free ssl hosting india shared plans" and landed here, here is the straight answer first: **all three major shared hosting providers in India — Hostinger, MilesWeb and Bluehost — include free SSL on every shared plan.** You do not pay extra for it, and no host here charges separately for a basic certificate.

This comparison is based on public specs and official pricing data — all three hosts' official pages were checked live on 6 November 2026, and every figure below was re-verified that day. Nothing here was bought or personally tested, so you will find no "I tested it myself" claim — only what the official pages actually say, and where those pages contradict each other, the contradiction is stated openly.

What SSL does is worth understanding first: it encrypts the connection between your website and a visitor's browser. That puts a padlock in the address bar, turns `http://` into `https://`, and stops the browser from showing a "Not Secure" warning. Running a serious website without SSL today is barely possible — people will not fill in forms, Chrome will warn them away, and payments are out of the question.

## Free SSL vs paid SSL: when is it worth paying extra?

Free SSL is usually a Domain Validated (DV) certificate. It verifies that the domain is yours, and gives visitors the padlock and HTTPS. For a blog, portfolio, business site or small online store, that is enough — visitors cannot tell whether the certificate was free or paid; they only see the padlock.

Consider paid SSL when:

- **You need a wildcard:** one certificate securing `blog.example.com`, `shop.example.com` and every other subdomain.
- **You need multi-domain:** one certificate covering several different domains.
- **You need Organization Validation:** your company name shown inside the certificate (mainly for large brands and financial sites).

MilesWeb's own blog lists its paid SSL prices as: single-domain DV SSL ₹799/year, multi-domain SAN SSL ₹2,999/year, wildcard DV SSL ₹5,999/year. Those figures are only here to show what paid SSL costs — for an ordinary blog, that spend is not necessary. And one thing to be clear about: **buying paid SSL does not make your website faster or rank higher** — it only changes the certificate type; the basic level of security is the same in free SSL.

## Four things to check even after free SSL is included

SSL being included and SSL working correctly are two different things. After buying hosting, run this checklist:

1. **Turn on the HTTPS redirect.** Enable force-HTTPS in your hosting panel or WordPress settings, so that even `http://` visitors land on `https://`. Without the redirect, both versions stay live and Google may treat them as duplicates.
2. **Confirm auto-renewal.** Free SSL is usually a 90-day certificate that auto-renews. Check the expiry date in your panel — if auto-renewal fails, your site will show a warning.
3. **Fix mixed content.** If the page loads over HTTPS but an image or script still loads over `http://`, the browser shows a warning alongside the padlock. In WordPress, a plugin like Really Simple SSL or a search-replace fixes this.
4. **Check the padlock yourself.** Once the site is live, open it in your browser and look — is the padlock there, or does it say "Not Secure"? That 30-second test is more reliable than any hosting company's claim.
