## The 48-month math: intro price vs renewal

Hostinger's pricing follows the industry pattern — **low intro price, much higher renewal**. Run the numbers before you buy:

**Single plan — 4 years**
- Intro (48 months): ₹69 × 48 = **₹3,312**
- After renewal: ₹289/mo (₹220/mo more than intro)

**Premium plan — 4 years**
- Intro (48 months): ₹149 × 48 = **₹7,152**
- After renewal: ₹449/mo (₹300/mo more than intro)

**Difference over 4 years**
- Total: ₹7,152 − ₹3,312 = **₹3,840**
- Per month: ₹3,840 ÷ 48 = **just ₹80/mo**

That ₹80/month buys real capacity: two extra websites, 10 GB → 20 GB storage, and an unambiguous free domain for a year. If there is even a small chance you will run a second project, that is cheap insurance.

The warning: **the renewal jump is steep.** Single goes ₹69 → ₹289/mo (**4.2× jump**) and Premium goes ₹149 → ₹449/mo (**3× jump**). The smart strategy is to take the 48-month term and lock the intro rate for as long as possible. Four years is a long time for a website — long enough that the site should be earning before renewal arrives.

## The GST line item nobody budgets for

Hostinger India's prices exclude **18% GST, added at checkout**. Your ₹69/mo Single plan is actually about ₹81.42/mo at payment (before any cart-level discounts), and the 48-month Single total rises accordingly. This is standard across Indian hosting, not a Hostinger trick — but it catches buyers by surprise.

The practical side: if you are a **GST-registered business** (not under the composition scheme), add your GSTIN in the billing/tax section at checkout so you receive a proper tax invoice and can claim the 18% as input tax credit — making it effectively cost-neutral. Composition-scheme businesses and individuals get no credit; budget the full 18%.

## Which Hostinger plan is right for you?

### Choose Single if:

- You need **exactly one website** — a blog, a portfolio, or a small business site
- Your budget is tight and every rupee counts
- You are building your first website and still experimenting
- 10 GB SSD is enough (a typical WordPress blog runs comfortably in 2–5 GB)

**Example:** Rohan is starting his first tech blog. He has one domain idea and roughly ₹3,500 of budget. Single at ₹3,312 for 4 years (plus GST at checkout) is the perfect fit — no wasted capacity.

### Choose Premium if:

- You have **2–3 website ideas** (blog + portfolio + a client's project, or Hindi + English versions)
- You plan to expand — one site today, another in six months
- You want 20 GB of storage (more images, a slightly heavier site)
- You want the free domain for a year stated clearly, with no ambiguity

**Example:** Priya runs a food blog and wants to launch a second site selling her recipe ebook. Buying two separate Single plans would cost more than one Premium plan that covers 3 sites.

### Quick decision rule

> **"One site, for sure? → Single. Even thinking about a second site? → Premium."**

At ₹80/month of difference, a second project anywhere in your future makes Premium the cheaper option — buying a separate plan later costs more than buying Premium now.

## The renewal trap: what hosting companies don't advertise

Hostinger is not alone — almost every shared host charges a low intro price and a much higher renewal. But Hostinger's jump is among the most visible:

- **Single:** ₹69 → ₹289/mo (**4.2×**)
- **Premium:** ₹149 → ₹449/mo (**3×**)

This does not make Hostinger bad. It means **you must plan for renewal from day one.** Four practical tips:

1. **Take the 48-month term.** It locks the intro rate for the longest time. Shorter terms cost more per month and bring renewal sooner.
2. **Note your renewal date in a calendar.** Check for retention offers 1–2 months before — existing customers sometimes get a discount.
3. **Set an earning target for the 4 years.** If your site cannot cover ₹449/month after four years, the problem is the strategy, not the hosting price.
4. **Ask support politely at renewal time.** Negotiating a renewal discount over chat is a common and legitimate practice.

One more honest point: some people consider switching hosts at renewal to dodge the price. Migration takes effort and carries downtime risk. Choosing the right term on day one is almost always cheaper and safer than migrating later.

Good news: **Hostinger supports plan upgrades.** You can move from Single to Premium (or Premium to Unlimited) later through hPanel, and the price difference is adjusted. Nothing you built is lost — everything stays in the same server environment.

- **But do the math:** if you already know a second site is coming within months, buying Premium from day one is cheaper than upgrading later at prevailing rates.
