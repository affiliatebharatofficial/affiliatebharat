Opening your first demat account is the gateway to investing in Indian stocks — and the choice feels harder than it is. Four discount brokers dominate the beginner conversation: Zerodha, Groww, Upstox and Angel One. This guide compares them on verified charges only (official pages, checked 2026-10-09), based on analysis of public specs and pricing data — not personal testing.

**This article is for information only, not financial advice. Do your own research before investing.**

## What a demat account actually is (30-second version)

A demat account holds your shares electronically (like a bank account holds money). You also need a **trading account** (to place buy/sell orders) — all four brokers below give you both together. You do **not** need a demat account for mutual funds.

## Beyond charges: what actually differs for beginners

**Zerodha** — India's largest broker by clients. ₹0 delivery is the headline; Varsity (its free investing education) is genuinely excellent for beginners. App is powerful but can feel dense on day one.

**Groww** — the simplest onboarding of the four and ₹0 AMC forever. Originally a mutual-fund app, so it is natural if you already invest in mutual funds there. Delivery brokerage is not zero, but the app experience is the friendliest.

**Upstox** — clean, modern app; flat ₹20 delivery is simple to understand. Backed by major investors. Good middle ground.

**Angel One** — the only one of the four with full-service DNA (research reports, advisory, SmartAPI). Brokerage is flat and low (₹20 or 0.1% capped). Worth considering if you want guidance alongside the account.

## 5 things to check before opening any demat account

1. **Delivery brokerage** — you will do mostly delivery trades; Zerodha's ₹0 wins here.
2. **AMC from year two** — Groww ₹0; Angel One ₹240; Zerodha/Upstox ₹300 + GST.
3. **Hidden charges** — DP charges on selling (~₹15–20 per scrip per day, industry-wide), payment gateway fees, and call-and-trade charges exist at all brokers. Read the full charge list, not just the headline.
4. **App reliability during market hours** — check recent user reviews for downtime complaints; a cheap broker that freezes at 9:15 AM is expensive.
5. **Support quality** — as a beginner you *will* have questions (nominee addition, DIS slips, corporate actions). Test support responsiveness before you fund the account.

## Beginner mistakes to avoid

- **Starting with intraday or F&O.** The brokerage tables above make F&O look cheap per trade. The losses are not in the brokerage.
- **Opening multiple accounts at once.** One good account is enough for your first year.
- **Investing before learning basics.** Spend a weekend on Zerodha Varsity or equivalent free material first.
- **Ignoring the AMC.** ₹300/year sounds small until you hold a ₹5,000 portfolio — that is 6% of your money gone annually.
- **Following "tips" from social media.** No broker's research — free or paid — replaces your own judgment.

## Bottom line

- **Lowest cost for buy-and-hold:** Zerodha (₹0 delivery) — but remember ₹300/yr AMC from year two.
- **Simplest + cheapest to maintain:** Groww (₹0 AMC, friendliest app).
- **Best flat-rate simplicity:** Upstox (₹20/order across segments).
- **Best if you want research/advisory:** Angel One (flat low brokerage + full-service features, ₹240/yr AMC after free year one).

Pick one, start small, and invest regularly. The broker matters far less than the habit.

A final word of caution that applies to every broker on this list: your account opening is free, but your mistakes are not. Paper-trade or invest tiny amounts for your first three to six months while you learn how orders, settlements and corporate actions actually work. The investors who survive their first year are the ones who treated it as tuition — cheap tuition, paid deliberately, rather than expensive tuition paid by accident.
