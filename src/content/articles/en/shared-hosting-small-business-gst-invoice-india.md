When a shop, clinic, salon or consultancy in India gets a website, choosing hosting is not just a "which plan is cheapest" question. If your business is **GST-registered**, the 18% GST on the hosting bill can be claimed back as **input tax credit (ITC)** — effectively making that 18% cost-neutral. But only on one condition: you must hold a **proper GST tax invoice** with your **GSTIN** printed on it.

Pick the wrong vendor or misconfigure your billing once, and that 18% becomes a real extra cost every year — not a small amount for a small business. This guide sticks to verified public data: which hosts issue GST invoices properly, whether displayed prices include GST or not, and the correct process for claiming ITC.

## Why 18% GST applies to web hosting in India

In India, web hosting is a **service**, and IT services carry an **18% GST rate**. The September 2025 slab changes (which removed the 12% and 28% brackets) left the IT services rate untouched at 18%.

The thing that confuses nearly every small business owner is **displayed price vs checkout price**. Our verified research found a consistent pattern:

- **MilesWeb** states plainly in its official FAQ: *"All MilesWeb hosting prices shown are exclusive of GST. An 18% GST is added at checkout."*
- **Hostinger India**'s displayed prices also exclude GST according to third-party sources — 18% is added at checkout (this could not be confirmed directly from the official pricing page, so treat it as third-party information).
- **Bluehost**'s official tax help page says 18% GST is collected from India customers.

The practical rule: **multiply the displayed monthly price by 1.18 to get your real cost** when choosing a plan — or, if you're GST-registered, build your ITC claim plan (details below).

## How GST invoices and input tax credit actually work

The concept is simple, but four conditions must all hold. Miss one and the credit is gone:

1. **GST-registered business:** you must hold a valid GSTIN.
2. **Not under the composition scheme:** composition-scheme businesses cannot claim ITC (see the FAQ above).
3. **Business use:** the hosting must serve your business — hosting bought for a personal blog doesn't qualify.
4. **Proper tax invoice:** the invoice must carry your GSTIN and appear in your **GSTR-2B**. That happens only when the vendor files its **GSTR-1** on time.

An example makes it concrete: you buy hosting worth ₹10,000 (pre-GST). At checkout, 18% GST = ₹1,800 is added; total bill ₹11,800. If all four conditions above are met, that ₹1,800 offsets against your output tax — the hosting's **effective cost is just ₹10,000**. This is why "cheapest plan" means something different for GST-registered businesses than for everyone else.

**The single most important practical tip:** enter your GSTIN in the hosting account's billing settings the moment you sign up. Adding a GSTIN to old invoices later is painful and usually impossible. When your first invoice arrives, check immediately that your GSTIN is printed on it.

## Red flags: spot these at checkout or on the invoice

- **GST was charged but your GSTIN isn't printed on the invoice:** such an invoice is useless for ITC. Demand a corrected tax invoice from the vendor — and avoid vendors that won't issue one.
- **The quote said "all inclusive", then 18% appeared at checkout:** a common trick. Confirm in writing at the quote stage whether the price is GST-inclusive or exclusive.
- **A cash discount with no bill:** some small vendors offer "cheaper without a bill". You lose **three things** — the ITC, the expense deduction (you can't book the expense under income tax), and clean proof of ownership of your domain/hosting. Never worth it for a business asset.

## A 5-step checklist for buying hosting with GST compliance

1. **Keep your GSTIN ready before signup.** Enter it in the billing/tax settings as you create the account — "I'll do it later" is the most common and most expensive mistake.
2. **Verify the GST breakup at checkout.** CGST 9% + SGST 9% (or IGST 18%) should be shown clearly. If you see only a lump-sum total, take a screenshot and confirm the tax invoice with support.
3. **Check your GSTIN on the first invoice.** It must be printed on the invoice. If it's missing, write to support immediately — corrections are easiest in the first billing cycle.
4. **Save every invoice and confirm ITC with your CA.** Get your CA/accountant to verify the invoice is reflecting in your GSTR-2B — especially the first time.
5. **Write down the renewal price.** Set a reminder for the renewal date. With hosts like Hostinger the renewal is 3x — that moment is when guides like our [Hostinger plan comparison](/en/hostinger-single-vs-premium-plan/) make it easy to re-evaluate.
