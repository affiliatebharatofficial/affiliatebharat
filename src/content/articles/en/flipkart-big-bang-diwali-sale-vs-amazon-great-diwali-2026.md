## Red flags: what to watch out for

- **"Expected" waves are not a strategy.** The 1–8 November Amazon wave our Hinglish article called "expected" turned out to be a real sale that started 28 October — under a different name (Diwali Edition) and a different bank partner (HDFC). If a tracker dates a sale, treat it as a rumour until the platform announces it.
- **"Up to 80–90% off" is measured against MRP.** The real deal is where the current price sits clearly below the last 30–60 days of price history. Use a price-history tool on Amazon and Flipkart's own price trend before trusting a banner.
- **Exchange value's "up to" trap.** The advertised exchange value assumes a best-condition device. Your phone's actual value is quoted at checkout and finalised after a pickup-time condition check. Back up data and factory-reset before pickup.
- **Caps are per card, per period.** Flipkart's SBI cap resets each offer period — if you exhaust it, another eligible SBI card or the next period (the last runs 6–10 November) restores it. Amazon's HDFC cap is unstated — never assume it.
- **Fake urgency.** Countdowns often reset when a wave changes. Judge the price and the bank offer, not the timer.
