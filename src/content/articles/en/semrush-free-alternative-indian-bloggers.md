For an Indian blogger, the biggest SEO question is simple: **can you manage without a premium tool like Semrush, or not?** Semrush's entry-level SEO plan costs $117.33 a month even on annual billing — and $139 a month if you pay monthly. Semrush bills in US dollars, so your card's exchange rate and forex markup decide the final rupee figure, and you need a card with international transactions enabled.

This guide looks honestly at what Semrush really costs, how far its free account actually goes, and which free tools together cover how much of its work. Everything here is based on an analysis of public specs, official pricing pages and third-party pricing coverage — no "we personally tested it" claims, because we have not bought and run these tools as a customer.

**Honesty note:** Where an official page did not confirm a figure, it is labelled as reported, not stated as fact. Prices are in US dollars unless stated otherwise.

## Semrush's free account: how far does it go?

Before paying anything, Semrush offers a **free account** — no credit card, no expiry date. According to its official pricing and free-account pages, you get:

- **10 reports a day** — Domain Overview, Keyword Research, Competitor Research, Backlinks — 10 queries a day across these tools
- **1 project** (1 website, listed as a demo project) and **10 tracked keywords**
- **Site Audit: up to 100 pages**
- Limited access to SEO Ideas, plus Help Center and basic onboarding

That is plenty for a blogger who checks one keyword or one competitor two or three times a week. It is not a daily workflow: 10 lookups will run out by midday, and tracking only 10 keywords cannot cover a growing blog.

The **7-day free trial** is a different thing: it gives you a full paid plan for 7 days, but it needs a credit card and auto-bills when the week ends. Only start a trial when a week's worth of SEO work is already ready. And remember the unverified warning from the FAQ: some third-party guides say that after a trial or paid plan, you may lose eligibility for the free account. Semrush's official page does not confirm this, so treat it as a risk to keep in mind, not a confirmed rule.

## Free alternative #1: Google Search Console (the most important one)

If you could pick only one free tool, pick **Google Search Console (GSC)**. It is Google's own official tool, completely free, with no usage limit. It gives you:

- Which keywords your site shows up for in Google, and which ones actually get clicks
- Which pages are indexed and which have errors
- Mobile usability and Core Web Vitals issues
- Which sites link to you

**GSC's limit:** it only shows data for your own verified sites. It will not tell you which keyword a competitor ranks for, how difficult a keyword is, or suggest new keyword ideas. So use GSC as the base, and add a keyword tool on top of it.

One practical note: GSC's dashboard sits behind a Google login and shows your own site's private data, so there is no screenshot of it in this article — its public page is linked here: {{aff:google-search-console|Google Search Console}}.

## Free alternative #2: Ubersuggest's free tier

Neil Patel's **Ubersuggest** is the most popular budget tool among Indian bloggers, because its interface is simple and even the free tier gives useful data. According to third-party pricing coverage from October 2026, the free plan gives you:

- **3 searches a day** — keyword ideas, site audit and domain overview all draw from the same 3
- **1 project** and around **25 tracked keywords**
- A **free Chrome extension** that shows keyword data on Google, YouTube and Amazon results (roughly 40 overlay searches a day, per third-party coverage)

Ubersuggest's strength is how much data each search returns: monthly search volume, SEO difficulty score, paid difficulty, content ideas and SERP analysis. Its weakness is that 3 searches a day run out fast, and its data on long-tail keywords is generally considered less deep than Semrush's.

Paid plans, per the same coverage, start at $29 a month for Individual (around 150 searches a day and 125 tracked keywords), with a lifetime deal also offered — confirm current prices on the official pricing page before buying, since promotions change them.

## What free tools honestly cannot replace

The free stack covers a lot, but some Semrush features have no free equivalent — and knowing that matters, so you do not build false expectations:

- **Keyword Gap at scale:** comparing your site against a competitor across hundreds of keywords at once does not exist in free tools.
- **Historical data:** seeing which keyword a competitor ranked for two years ago is a Pro+ level feature.
- **Content optimisation toolkit:** real-time SEO suggestions while you write an article (Pro+ and above) has no free version.
- **Backlink database depth:** Semrush's backlink index is considered among the deepest in the industry; free tools show your own site's links fully, but not a competitor's.
- **API and white-label reporting:** needed for client work, not available free.

One bonus free option: **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools** — free backlink data and a site audit of up to 5,000 pages for your own verified sites, according to third-party coverage. It gives no competitor data either, but it pairs well with GSC for your own site's backlinks.

**Stage 3 — Regular blog income, or client work:**
