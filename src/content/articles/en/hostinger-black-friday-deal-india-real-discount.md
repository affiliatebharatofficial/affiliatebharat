## The sale is live — but "Up to 80% OFF" is not the whole price

Hostinger's India homepage on **26 October 2026** carries a live festive banner: **"Sale Season — Up to 80% OFF — Get your idea online today from ₹139/mo."** Five days ago, when the Hinglish original of this article was published, the same hero read "Up to 83% OFF". The headline has moved; the plan prices behind it have not. That small shift is exactly why this English version re-checks the numbers instead of translating them: in sale season, the banner is the least stable number on the page.

The honest answer to the title question: yes, the discount is real, but it applies to the first term only, and the headline percentage is measured against a struck-through list price. Below are the verified plan prices, the renewal prices printed on the same cards, the GST-inclusive totals, and the one alternative worth comparing — all with dates, so you can tell what was checked today from what was captured on 21 October.

**Honesty note:** This article is based on official pricing pages and public data analysed — not on any claim of personal testing. Where a figure comes from a deal tracker rather than the brand's own page, it is labelled as such. The checkout page is always the final authority.

## How long will the sale run? (Honest framing)

Three facts, kept separate:

- **The banner is live today (26 October 2026)** — verified on hostinger.com/in this morning.
- **No end date is announced on the official page.** Hostinger has not printed an "ends on" date anywhere on its homepage or pricing page.
- **The tracker consensus is 19 October to 14 December 2026.** Multiple independent trackers (hostingguider, elitecontentmarketer, googiehost, digitalprahlad, vpsdex) give that window, which covers Black Friday (27 November 2026) and Cyber Monday (30 November 2026).

That means the sale is already running and, if the trackers are right, will still be running on Black Friday itself. There is no verified "last chance" pressure — but equally, no verified guarantee that waiting until 27 November gets you a better price. Judge the deal on today's numbers, not on a countdown that nobody official published.

## Coupon codes: what is true and what is not

Several coupon sites claim an extra 10% off with a BLACKFRIDAY code, or quote Premium at ₹129/mo, or promise "3 months free". The position, verified against the official page:

- Hostinger's own page mentions **no coupon code** — festive prices apply automatically.
- Those code claims are **aggregator-reported and officially unverified**. Some may work at checkout, some are long expired.
- The rule is simple: try a code if you like, but **the checkout page's final order summary is the only real price**. A blog's claim is not a discount.

## Should you wait for 27 November?

**The case against waiting:** the sale is live, prices are sale prices, and no verified source promises a lower price on Black Friday itself. Every week of waiting is also a week of lost indexing time if your site is ready to launch. And there is a safety net — a 30-day money-back guarantee on eligible plans (domain registration itself is not refundable, which is standard).

**The one case for waiting:** if your project genuinely cannot launch before late November, waiting costs nothing — the tracker window runs to mid-December. But wait because your project needs it, not because a countdown told you to.

## The three-number formula for any festive deal

Whatever host you are evaluating, you only need three numbers:

1. **Intro total:** monthly intro × term months. This is what the sale actually discounts.
2. **Renewal rate:** the per-month price after the term ends. Whether a host publishes it is itself a quality signal — Hostinger does, MilesWeb only partially, Bluehost and BigRock do not.
3. **GST:** 18% on Indian invoices. "Prices listed without GST" is not small print you can skip.

Run those three numbers through the [hosting comparison tool](/tools/hosting-compare/) and the [website cost calculator](/tools/website-cost-calculator/) before you pay, and compare the five-year total — not the hero percentage.
