## What a salon website actually needs from hosting

Before comparing plans, it helps to know what the website will actually do — because the answer rules out both overkill and underpowered choices:

- **Booking or enquiry channel:** a WhatsApp chat button or a simple booking form, so customers can book an appointment in one tap. This is the single most important feature.
- **Photo gallery:** haircuts, bridal makeup, facial results — salon work is visual. Thirty to fifty high-quality photos is normal, so you want some storage headroom.
- **Google Maps embed:** for "salon near me" searches, location is the biggest conversion factor. The map needs to load fast.
- **Fast mobile loading:** 80%+ of your customers will arrive on a phone. If the site takes more than 3 seconds to load, they go back to Google and open the next salon's website.
- **SSL certificate:** the booking form collects names and phone numbers — without SSL, browsers show a "Not Secure" warning. Both recommended hosts include free SSL.

All of this runs comfortably on shared hosting. A VPS or "business cloud" plan would be wasted money for 95% of salons — spend that budget on marketing instead.

## How much traffic does a salon website really get?

Realistic expectations keep you from overbuying:

- **New salon (first 6 months):** 200–800 visitors/month — mostly from Google Maps and "salon near me" searches.
- **Established local salon:** 1,000–5,000 visitors/month — repeat customers plus Instagram/Facebook traffic.
- **Multiple branches or bridal specialist:** 5,000–20,000 visitors/month — still within shared hosting's comfort zone.

Shared hosting typically handles **10,000–25,000 visits per month** without breaking a sweat. Only when you know heavy traffic is coming does it make sense to pay for more.

## 5 things to check before buying hosting for your salon

Read this checklist once before you enter card details — these are the points where first-time website owners usually get stuck:

1. **Understand the billing term.** The ₹149/mo price only applies when you pay for the 48-month term upfront — roughly ₹7,150 on day one. Shorter terms cost more per month. Choose the term that fits your cash flow, not just the headline price.

2. **Check the money-back policy.** MilesWeb offers a 30-day money-back guarantee — if the service doesn't suit you in the first month, you can get a refund. Reading refund terms before buying is never wasted time.

3. **Do the GST math.** Both hosts add 18% GST at checkout — MilesWeb's ₹99/mo works out to about ₹117/mo in reality. It is a small amount, but put it in your business budget now so your books don't surprise you later.

4. **Note the free domain's expiry.** The free domain covers year one only; from year two it costs ~₹1,000–1,500/year. Set a reminder 15 days before renewal — recovering an expired domain is expensive and messy.

5. **Don't rely on the host's backup alone.** Hosts provide weekly or daily backups, but download your own copy once a month — it takes five minutes and saves you one day when you need it, especially before making big changes.

## DIY vs developer: build it yourself or hire someone?

The question every salon owner asks, answered honestly:

**Build it yourself (DIY) — when:**
- You are comfortable with basic computer work and learning from YouTube
- The site is simple: services, prices, gallery, WhatsApp button, map
- Your budget is limited to ₹2,000–3,000/year
- A decent salon site takes 1–2 days with WordPress + a free theme + a WhatsApp chat plugin

**Hire a developer — when:**
- You have zero time and want to focus on the business
- You need a proper booking system with advance payments
- You want a professional design immediately

There is also a middle path: **start DIY in year one** (only the hosting cost), then hire a developer for an upgrade once bookings grow. By then you will also know exactly how many customers the website brings in — which makes the developer's fee easy to justify.

One non-negotiable, whichever route you take: **buy the hosting in your own name.** It is disturbingly common for a developer to buy hosting on their own account, disappear, and leave the site stranded. Domain and hosting must always stay under the owner's control.
