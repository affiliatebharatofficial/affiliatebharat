## The short answer: no UPI, no RuPay either

You have heard good things about A2 Hosting — Turbo speed, LiteSpeed servers, strong reviews — and you are ready to buy. Then one question stops you: **can you pay with UPI?** You only have UPI, no international card, and getting stuck at checkout is nobody's idea of a good start.

Here is the direct answer, and it comes from the company's own documents, not from another blog's copied list: **no, you cannot pay A2 Hosting with UPI.** But the story does not end there — because some websites claim the exact opposite, and A2 Hosting has also changed its name. Both facts matter, or you could buy the wrong host or get your money stuck at checkout. This article is based on public specs, the official billing policy and pricing pages, all verified live on 12 November 2026. Nothing here was bought or personally tested, so you will find no "I tested it myself" claim — only what the official pages actually say, including where they contradict themselves.

## The official proof: what Hosting.com's billing policy actually says

A2 Hosting's own policy — now published under the hosting.com name — lists every accepted payment method in Section 6, "Payment Methods; Currencies", of its Refund & Billing Policy:

**Visa, MasterCard, Discover, American Express, JCB, Diners Club, PayPal, Skrill, UnionPay (for China-based customers only), cheque, money order, and wire transfer.**

That list contains **no UPI. No RuPay. No net banking. No Google Pay or PhonePe.** A search of the whole policy for those terms returns no matches at all. The same section says billing is in US dollars — "We accept US Dollar and limited non-USD currencies" — and that non-USD prices are converted at the exchange rate posted that day, which "may differ from your final rate given to you by your bank".

This is not an old page. The policy was re-read live on 12 November 2026, and Section 1 still states: "Hosting.com is operated by A2 Hosting, LLC." Same company, new name.

## First, clear up the confusion: A2 Hosting is now Hosting.com

If you are reading older articles you will see the name "A2 Hosting" everywhere, with Startup, Drive and Turbo plans. **In April 2025, A2 Hosting rebranded as Hosting.com.** Before that, in January 2025, World Host Group acquired the company. The old a2hosting.com address now redirects to hosting.com.

What that means in practice:

- **There is no separate "A2 Hosting India" company or Indian storefront.** Any site selling "A2 Hosting India" is either using the old name or running an affiliate page.
- Today's plan names have changed too — shared hosting now comes as **Starter, Pro and Max** (5, 10 and 50 websites), not the old Startup/Drive names.
- The homepage shows a location selector reading "United States (Global)" — you are buying from the global (US) storefront, not an Indian one.

The rebrand matters because the UPI confusion starts here.

## The conflict: some blogs say "UPI works on A2" — why that is wrong

A popular claim on the internet says "A2 Hosting India accepts UPI via Razorpay, shared hosting from ₹213/month". It appears on an affiliate blog. Three strong pieces of evidence go against it:

1. **UPI is not in the official policy.** The list above does not contain UPI at all. No company hides a payment method from its own billing policy.
2. **An independent payment-page check, 6 November 2026.** A second site checked the official payment pages of several hosts on 6 November 2026 and wrote plainly: "Hosting.com (formerly A2 Hosting): No UPI — card, PayPal and bank transfer." The same check marks UPI as "Yes" for Hostinger, MilesWeb and BigRock — so the checker recognised UPI, it simply was not there on Hosting.com.
3. **There is no Indian storefront.** UPI can only be offered by a host with an Indian payment gateway — like Hostinger India or MilesWeb, which bill in rupees. Hosting.com bills in dollars from a US storefront. An Indian gateway like Razorpay does not appear anywhere in that checkout.

So this article's position is clear: **the "UPI on A2 via Razorpay" claim is not supported by any official source, and you should not buy a plan relying on it.** If you see it on a page, that page is either old or written to earn a commission.

## No UPI — so how do you pay Hosting.com?

UPI not working does not mean an Indian buyer cannot buy Hosting.com at all. These are the routes:

**1. A card with international transactions switched on.** A Visa or MasterCard credit or debit card with international transactions enabled. Checkout happens in dollars, and your bank converts it to rupees — conversion charges and a forex markup (typically around 3–3.5%) may apply on top. Switch international transactions on in your bank's app before buying, or the payment will be declined.

**2. PayPal.** Hosting.com accepts PayPal. You can pay from a PayPal account or through guest checkout with a card. PayPal applies its own conversion rate, which can be slightly more expensive than a bank's — check the final amount at checkout before paying.

**3. Skrill or wire transfer.** Both are listed in the policy, but neither is practical for most bloggers — Skrill means another account and its own fees, and wire transfers carry high bank charges.

**4. Cheque or money order.** Also listed in the policy, but completely impractical from India — do not waste time on these.

One more thing to know: **all Hosting.com plans and domains are set to auto-renew** — that is in Section 2 of the same policy. Payment is attempted 3 days before the due date. So your card must still be valid and have international transactions switched on at renewal time, or the service can be suspended. Renewal prices are covered in the next section.

## Which host should you choose? A decision guide

- **You only have UPI, no international card:** Hostinger or MilesWeb. Both accept UPI, RuPay and net banking. Hostinger's Premium at ₹139/mo is the most balanced plan; MilesWeb's Starter at ₹69/mo is the cheapest entry point.
- **You need a GST invoice (for a business):** MilesWeb or Hostinger. MilesWeb promises a GST invoice on its homepage; Hostinger issues one too.
- **You want Hosting.com's speed (LiteSpeed + NVMe + Turbo):** Only if you have an international card or PayPal. Trying to pay by UPI will leave you stuck at checkout, and the renewal will not auto-charge either.
- **Cheapest first year:** Hosting.com's Black Friday Starter at $17.88 a year (roughly ₹1,575 before bank conversion) — but remember the second year at $167.88 a year. MilesWeb's Starter at ₹69/mo + GST for 36 months (₹2,484 + GST) renewing at ₹149/mo gives a far more predictable 3-year total.
- **You want Hindi support:** MilesWeb states "Support in English or Hindi, 24/7"; Hostinger's support is also available in Hindi.

## Practical steps for paying by UPI

1. **Pick a UPI host** — Hostinger, MilesWeb or BigRock from the table above.
2. **Choose a plan and term** — a longer term (36 or 48 months) works out cheaper, but check the renewal price too. The intro price applies only to the first term.
3. **Select UPI at checkout** — approve the payment from GPay, PhonePe, Paytm or your BHIM UPI app. Note that UPI AutoPay does not always work for renewals — some banks do not support it — so mark the renewal date in your calendar and renew manually.
4. **Download the GST invoice** — for business use, take the GST invoice from the invoice section after checkout.
5. **Paying by international card instead?** — switch international transactions on in your bank's app first, and check both the USD amount shown at checkout and your bank's conversion rate. The forex markup means the final amount on your card can be slightly higher than the converted headline price.
